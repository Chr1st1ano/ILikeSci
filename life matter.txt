LESSON 1: Scientific Inquiry in Life Science
Learning Competency
The learners can:
Perform simple guided investigations using the skills of observing, predicting, and measuring to gather data and answer questions about the natural world.
LET'S EXPLORE
Have you ever wondered why a plant grows toward sunlight? Or why some animals behave differently when they are in different places?
Scientists ask questions about things they observe. They use simple investigations to find answers.
WHAT IS SCIENTIFIC INQUIRY?
Scientific inquiry is a way of learning about the natural world by asking questions, observing, making predictions, gathering information, and using evidence.
For young scientists, three important skills are:
Skill
Meaning
Example
Observing
Using our senses or tools to gather information
Looking at the leaves of a plant
Predicting
Making a reasonable guess about what may happen
Predicting that a plant near sunlight will grow well
Measuring
Using tools or standard units to determine something
Measuring the height of a plant

SIMPLE INVESTIGATION
A simple investigation may follow these steps:
Ask → Predict → Observe → Measure → Record → Explain
Example
Question: Does a plant grow taller over time?
Prediction: The plant will become taller after several days.
Observation: The plant is measured each day.
Measurement: Its height is recorded in centimeters.
Conclusion: The observations and measurements are used to explain what happened.
MULTIMEDIA SUPPORT

KEY TAKEAWAYS
Scientists ask questions about the natural world.
Observing means gathering information.
Predicting means making a reasonable guess about what may happen.
Measuring uses tools or standard units to determine something.
Scientists use evidence from observations and measurements to answer questions.
