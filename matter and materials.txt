
TERM 1
LESSON 1: Properties and Uses of Materials
Learning Competencies
Learners should be able to:
describe and compare the observable physical properties of solid materials, such as hardness, shininess, roughness, smoothness, flexibility, and stretchability;
explain how changing the shape or form of solid materials, such as shaping, pressing, hammering, joining, or cutting, can make them useful for different purposes; 
describe the observable properties and everyday uses of common metal materials in Philippine homes, schools, and communities, such as iron or steel, galvanized iron sheets or yero, aluminum, copper, and stainless steel. 
LET'S EXPLORE
Look around the classroom.
What objects can you see? What are they made of?
Materials have different properties that we can observe and describe.
DISCUSSION
Observable Properties of Materials
Hardness — how hard a material is
Shininess — how much light a material reflects
Roughness — having an uneven surface
Smoothness — having an even surface
Flexibility — ability to bend
Stretchability — ability to stretch when pulled
Examples:
Rock → hard
Metal spoon → shiny
Sandpaper → rough
Glass → smooth
Rubber → flexible
Rubber band → stretchable
CHANGING MATERIALS
The shape or form of a solid material can be changed by:
shaping • pressing • hammering • joining • cutting
These changes can make materials useful for different purposes.
METALS AROUND US
Common metals found in homes, schools, and communities include:
Iron or steel — chairs, gates, tools
Galvanized iron sheets or yero — roofing
Aluminum — containers and household objects
Copper — electrical wires
Stainless steel — spoons, forks, and other utensils
INTERACTIVE ACTIVITY
What Property Is It?
A rubber band becomes longer when pulled.
What property does it show?
A. Hardness
B. Shininess
C. Stretchability
D. Roughness
Answer: C — Stretchability
